The Interactive Planetary Free-Fall Engine

About This Simulation

This interactive engine simulates the 1-dimensional free-fall of an object towards a massive planetary body in a vacuum. It utilizes numerical integration to calculate real-time kinematics, compressing the flow of time so astronomical events can be observed in seconds. The physics engine is strictly governed by real-world SI units and the Conservation of Energy.

Governing Equations

  • Gravitational Force: F = G · (M · m) / r² (Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation calculates the pulling force, which exponentially increases as distance decreases).
  • Real-Time Acceleration: a = F / m (Newton's Second Law. Notice that the falling object's mass 'm' cancels out when setting the two equations equal, proving the Equivalence Principle).
  • Impact Velocity: v = √(2GM · (1/R - 1/(R+h))) (Derived from the Conservation of Energy, calculating the exact speed of impact by converting potential energy into kinetic energy).
  • Total Fall Time: t = √(r₀³ / 2GM) · [√((R/r₀) · (1 - R/r₀)) + arccos(√(R/r₀))] (Derived from Kepler's Laws, this analytically calculates the exact duration of the fall across a changing gravitational field).
